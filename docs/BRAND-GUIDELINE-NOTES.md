# Brand Guideline: what it actually says

Read 31 August 2026 from `beco interiors brand identity 160425 .pdf`, dated April 2025, in the
BECO PRODUCTS Drive folder. Until now the design decisions were made from a second hand summary.
This is the primary source.

---

## Colours: three, and only three

| Colour | Spec | Guideline's own words |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm Red** | Pantone Red C, C0 M100 Y100 K0 | "dynamic, demands attention, often in a bold and loud manner. Due to this, it is **frequently used as an accent** to draw attention" |
| **Charcoal Black** | Pantone Black 6 C, **C40 M0 Y0 K100** | "Has a **cooling counter** to our warming red colour" |
| **High-Vis White** | C0 M0 Y0 K0, pure white | "Used as a **highlight** where required" |

> "Our brand colours are strong and single minded. **Intentionally narrow**, we want to build
> strong brand association, particularly with our Beco Interiors Warm Red."

**Three consequences.**

**The prototype's gold `#C4972A` is out.** D2 previously said keep it if the guideline had no
secondary. The guideline has no secondary *on purpose*: "intentionally narrow" is a stated
position, so adding a fourth colour contradicts the brand rather than filling a gap.

**Charcoal Black is a cool black, not neutral.** 40% cyan is deliberate, described as a cooling
counter to the red. So the neutral ramp must be generated from a cool charcoal, not from a warm
or neutral grey. Getting this wrong is subtle and wrong in a way people feel rather than name.

**Red as accent is the brand's own instruction**, not our interpretation. The near monochrome
direction is what the guideline asks for.

## Typography

Three families, three weights described as black, bold and light.

- **F1 Display**: Regular, Bold, Wide
- **Titillium**: Regular, SemiBold

The guideline genuinely specifies F1 Display, so it is a real brand asset rather than a stray
file. **D3 still stands**: the supplied pack came from blogfonts.com, which is not a licensed
distribution of Formula One's corporate typeface, and Brightex would be the party serving it.
Worth telling Beco their guideline specifies a face they may need to license properly.

## Logo: three variants with different jobs

| Variant | Use |
|---|---|
| **Display** | "to be displayed, most comfortable taking centre stage. Use it boldly." Hero use |
| **Standard** | Default in our own channels. Navigation |
| **Micro** | "drawn with extra contrast to hold better at micro sizes." Very small sizes only |

**Clear space is 50% of the logo height on all sides.** A hard rule.

**Misuse rules that affect our layouts directly:**

- Do not recolour
- Do not rotate or distort
- **Do not place on busy backgrounds**
- **Do not place on backgrounds of similar colour to the logo**
- Do not use the Standard logo at very large display sizes, use Display
- Do not use the Display logo at small sizes, use Micro

The two bolded rules constrain the hero. A logo sitting over slab photography is a logo on a
busy background. Either it sits in the clear type area, or the nav bar becomes solid behind it
sooner than the transparent-on-scroll behaviour would suggest.

## Photography direction, which is prescriptive

Directly relevant to the shot brief already sent to Beco.

> "The aim is to always **hero the location**, and showcase the inspiring workspace."

> "Images with people should always focus on them **working or on the move**. This helps to make
> them less recognizable. We use different focus techniques such as **shallow depth of field or
> motion blur**."

> "Beco should use **authentic people in authentic situations**, but this should not be the
> focal point. The overall photograph should always focus on **the wider interior space**, not
> the people."

Two things follow. Application shots should favour wide interior context over tight product
crops, which suits the signature scroll section. And the brand explicitly wants **authentic**
imagery, which is an argument against generated renders and in favour of real installations,
the opposite of what the competitor is doing.

## Brand tone

> "We've produced these guidelines to illustrate **'Elegance'**."

Stated on page one. It is the brand's own word, not an interpretation.

## Two contradictions to raise with Beco

### 1. "10+ years" versus "new entrant"

The approved prototype's About page claims **"10+ Years in Nairobi"** and "Kenya's trusted
interior materials partner".

The guideline, dated April 2025, says:

> "Beco Interiors is a **new entrant into the market newly launched** in the East African
> region."

These cannot both be right. The prototype copy was written by Brightex without sight of this
document. **Needs confirming before the About page ships**, because an overstated claim on a
public site is a credibility risk, and "new entrant" is not a weakness worth hiding when the
product range is the actual story.

### 2. Lighting is in the brand identity but not in Drive

The strapline appears on **every page** of the guideline:

> "Sintered Stone  ·  Lighting  ·  Panels  ·  Accessories"

Signage artwork spells it out again: SINTERED STONE / LIGHTING / PANELS / ACCESSORIES. The
mission text describes "sintered stone home furnishing **& lighting solutions**".

So lighting is one of four pillars of the brand, and the approved prototype had a Lighting
category with 88 products.

But **there is no Lighting folder in Drive**, and D4 set the Drive folders as the taxonomy,
which drops Lighting from the site entirely.

**Shipping a site with no lighting, for a company whose own strapline says lighting, is a
mismatch worth a decision rather than a default.** Options: keep a Lighting category with a
designed empty state until photography arrives, or confirm the range has genuinely changed
since April 2025 and update the strapline usage accordingly.
